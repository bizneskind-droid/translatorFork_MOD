### ADMISSION THRESHOLD (GATEKEEPING)

This block overrides any instruction above that could be read as encouragement to
produce entries. Read it as the gate every candidate must pass.

**The default verdict for every candidate term is REJECT.** You are not building a
dictionary and you are not measured by how many entries you return. You are
guarding an existing, curated, hand-corrected terminological base. Every entry you
add becomes a binding constraint that a translator must obey for thousands of
chapters, and a bad constraint is far more expensive than a missing one.

**Burden of proof.** A term is admitted only when leaving it out would demonstrably
break consistency later. If the translator would almost certainly produce an
acceptable and stable rendering without your entry, the entry is noise. Do not
record a decision that merely confirms the obvious.

**All three conditions must hold simultaneously. If even one fails, REJECT:**

1. **Ambiguity.** A competent translator could reasonably render it in more than one
   way, and those variants would visibly clash if mixed across chapters.
2. **Consequence.** An inconsistent rendering would confuse the reader about
   identity, rank, affiliation, geography, or mechanics — not merely read less
   smoothly.
3. **Absence.** The term is genuinely missing from the supplied glossary context —
   not present under a different surface form, not covered by an existing entry, not
   a declined, abbreviated or partial variant of one.

**Do not speculate about recurrence.** You see only the current fragment, so you
cannot know whether a term appears again later in the book. Do not try to estimate
it, and do not justify an entry by claiming it "will probably return". Recurrence is
verified mechanically outside this prompt: every term you emit is checked against the
remaining chapters of the source and dropped if it does not actually reappear. Your
job is to judge whether a term is ambiguous, consequential and absent — nothing else.

**Hard rejects, regardless of the three conditions:**

- Anything already covered by an existing entry, including inflected forms, partial
  matches, and compounds whose parts are separately present.
- One-off descriptive phrases, situational epithets, ad-hoc nicknames used in a
  single line of dialogue.
- Ordinary vocabulary, generic objects, common actions and qualities.
- Numbers, measurements, dates, and units of any kind.
- Terms whose only justification is that they look important or exotic.
- Chapter titles, headers, and structural markup.
- A term you cannot assign a confident, stable target rendering to. If you are
  guessing, you are manufacturing a constraint out of a guess — reject it.

**Conflict rule.** If a candidate contradicts an existing entry, REJECT it. The
existing base wins. It may contain deliberate human decisions whose reasons are not
visible in this fragment, and silently overriding them is the worst outcome of this
task.

**Volume expectation.** For a typical fragment of an ongoing work whose base is
already mature, the correct answer is **zero to two entries**. An empty result is a
valid, successful, and frequently correct answer — return an empty JSON object and
nothing else when no candidate clears the gate. A long list is a symptom of failure,
not of thoroughness: it means the gate was not applied.

**Self-check before emitting.** For each surviving entry, state to yourself which of
the four conditions justifies it and what specifically would break without it. If
that justification is vague, drop the entry.
